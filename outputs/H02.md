# H02 — AFS Spectroscopy Assessment

## Customer Enquiry

Our dairy process handles both a relatively clear whey stream and an opaque milk concentrate. We want a pilot for composition monitoring, using laboratory protein and fat results where those variables are relevant. We can compare different measurement interfaces before choosing the installation. What first measurement concepts and comparison experiment would make sense for these two streams?

---

## Requirements & Key Facts

**Client-stated facts:**
- Two distinct dairy streams in the same process:
  - A relatively clear whey stream
  - An opaque milk concentrate
- Composition monitoring is the goal
- Laboratory protein and fat results are available as reference measurements (where relevant to each stream)
- Client is open to comparing different measurement interfaces before selecting
- This is a pilot/feasibility stage

**Key unknowns:**
- Which analytes are needed for each stream specifically (protein only for whey? fat and protein for concentrate? other components?)
- Whether the whey stream turbidity varies during production (D03 scenario warns of this) [S01 p.17-18]
- Temperature range of each stream at measurement point
- Flow conditions (static, flowing, pulsed)
- Physical access constraints at each measurement location
- Whether the same probe must handle both streams or separate installations are acceptable

---

## Clarification Questions

1. **Whey stream turbidity stability:** Does the whey stream remain consistently clear, or does turbidity change during production runs (e.g., fines carryover, batch transitions)? This determines whether a transmission-only geometry is sufficient or whether the probe must handle variable scattering [S01 p.17-18].

2. **Target analytes per stream:** For the whey stream, is protein the primary target? For the milk concentrate, are both fat and protein required, or are other components (lactose, total solids) also targets?

3. **Temperature at measurement points:** What is the typical temperature range for each stream? This affects probe material selection and may influence spectral baseline.

4. **Single probe or separate installations:** Is the goal to find one probe geometry that works for both streams, or are separate optimised installations acceptable?

5. **Process timing:** Are both streams available simultaneously for a pilot, or would measurements need to be scheduled separately?

---

## Recommendation

**Decision Status:** Provisional assessment

**Rationale:**

The two streams represent classic dairy spectroscopy scenarios with different optical properties, well-documented in AFS sources [S01 p.17-18]:

**1. Opaque milk concentrate → NIR Diffuse Reflection**
- Concentrated/opaque dairy products (yogurt, butter, whole milk concentrates) are suited to NIR diffuse reflection geometry with a flush-mounted probe [S01 p.17-18]
- Fat and protein both measurable; fat globule size and surface characteristics affect signal — scattering correction (MSC, SNV) required before PLS regression [S01 p.17]
- Assumption: concentrate is sufficiently opaque that transmission is not viable

**2. Clear whey stream → NIR Transmission or Transflection**
- Clear/semi-transparent dairy liquids (skim milk, whey, permeate) are suited to NIR transmission with 1–10 mm path length [S01 p.18]
- However, D03 scenario explicitly warns: if turbidity may change, probe geometry must handle both clear and turbid states [S01 p.17-18]
- A transflection probe can work for both low-fat and high-fat products as a combined solution [S01 p.18]
- Assumption: whey protein is the primary target; if turbidity is variable, transflection is safer than pure transmission

**Why NIR for both:**
- NIR is the established method for dairy composition (fat, protein) with laboratory reference calibration [S01 p.17-18]
- Fiber link: standard Vis-NIR silica fiber (350–2400 nm) is appropriate for NIR dairy applications [S02 p.6-7]

**Alternative methods to note (not primary recommendation):**
- MIR ATR, Raman, and Fluorescence are used in advanced dairy labs for higher molecular specificity [S01 p.18], but NIR is the natural first candidate given established dairy applications and the client's available reference measurements

## Measurement Concept

**Stream 1: Opaque milk concentrate**
- **Geometry:** NIR diffuse reflection probe, flush-mounted to avoid dead volume and fouling [S01 p.17]
- **Optical depth:** 0.5–1 mm (diffuse reflection into scattering medium) [S01 Table 1 p.6]
- **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fiber cable; robust industrial cables available up to 100–300 m if needed [S02 p.7]
- **Measurement location:** In-line or at-line bypass; flush mount preferred for viscous concentrate

**Stream 2: Clear/variable whey**
- **Geometry:** NIR transflection probe recommended over pure transmission, given D03 warning about turbidity variability [S01 p.17-18]
- **Optical depth:** 1–10 mm adjustable path length [S01 p.18, Table 1 p.6]
- **Fiber link:** Same Vis-NIR silica fiber family [S02 p.6-7]
- **Measurement location:** In-line flow cell or insertion probe

**Note:** Exact probe model codes, path lengths, and wetted materials require AFS confirmation based on process conditions (temperature, CIP compatibility, pressure rating).

---

## Risks & Uncertainty

- **Whey turbidity variation:** If the whey stream scattering changes significantly during production, a transmission-only probe may give inconsistent results; transflection mitigates this but adds complexity [S01 p.17-18]

- **Fat globule size and temperature effects:** NIR reflection on concentrate is sensitive to physical variation (particle/globule size, temperature, texture) which can mimic composition changes — scattering correction is essential [S01 p.17]

- **Calibration is matrix-specific:** A model built on one concentrate formulation may not transfer to a different product; calibration transfer across product changes can invalidate existing models [S01 p.10, 14-15]

- **Independent reference requirement:** Fat and protein each need independent laboratory reference results; if the reference method has its own uncertainty, this propagates into the calibration [S01 p.17]

- **Single-probe compromise:** If the client wants one probe geometry for both streams, performance on one stream may be suboptimal — this is a trade-off to evaluate in the pilot

- **Assumption not verified:** I have assumed both streams are accessible for pilot measurements with representative composition variation; if only narrow-range samples are available, the pilot cannot validate a full-range calibration

## Feasibility Plan

**Objective:** Determine which NIR probe geometry(ies) can reliably predict composition (protein, fat where relevant) for each dairy stream, using laboratory reference values.

### 1. Samples

- **Milk concentrate:** Collect samples spanning the expected fat and protein range in production (e.g., low/mid/high for each analyte). Include physical variation if relevant (temperature, batch source).
- **Whey stream:** Collect samples spanning protein concentration range. Critically, include samples at different turbidity levels if turbidity varies in production — do not assume the stream stays clear [S01 p.17-18].
- **Minimum sample count:** Depends on composition range and matrix complexity; a pilot with too few samples cannot validate a calibration. AFS lab test will advise on required sample numbers after reviewing composition ranges.

### 2. Reference measurements

- Laboratory protein determination (e.g., Kjeldahl or Dumas) for both streams
- Laboratory fat determination (e.g., Röse-Gottlieb or Gerber) for concentrate
- These must be independent of the spectroscopic measurement — the lab result is the reference, not a secondary spectroscopic method

### 3. Measurements to compare

Following AFS's multi-method lab test approach [S05]:

| Stream | Geometry options to test | Rationale |
|--------|-------------------------|-----------|
| Milk concentrate | NIR diffuse reflection probe | Established for opaque dairy [S01 p.17] |
| Whey | NIR transmission probe (fixed path) | If turbidity is confirmed stable |
| Whey | NIR transflection probe | Handles variable turbidity [S01 p.18] |
| Both (optional) | Transflection probe on both | Tests single-geometry compromise |

- Run all candidate geometries on representative samples in AFS multi-spectral lab
- Acquire spectra under controlled conditions; record sample temperature

### 4. Sequence

1. **Single-method models first:** Build separate PLS regression models for each stream × geometry combination, using scattering correction (MSC or SNV) as appropriate [S01 p.17]
2. **Compare prediction error:** Evaluate each geometry against laboratory reference values
3. **If justified:** Test whether one transflection probe can serve both streams with acceptable accuracy
4. **Combinations (e.g., NIR + Raman or NIR + Fluorescence):** Only pursue if single-method NIR does not meet accuracy requirements; AFS sources indicate these are used in advanced dairy labs [S01 p.18] but NIR is the natural first candidate

### 5. Go/no-go basis

- A numerical acceptance criterion (e.g., RMSEP for protein, RMSEP for fat, relative to laboratory reproducibility) must be agreed with the client before the experiment
- The criterion should reflect what prediction error is acceptable for process decisions
- I do not invent a number here — this is a client-specific decision

### 6. What this experiment demonstrates and does not demonstrate

**Demonstrates:**
- Whether NIR can discriminate composition variation in these specific streams
- Which probe geometry gives best prediction accuracy for each stream
- Whether a single geometry can serve both streams (if tested)

**Does NOT demonstrate:**
- Long-term calibration stability in production (requires extended validation)
- Performance under all process upsets (fouling, temperature excursions, CIP cycles)
- Transferability to different product formulations without recalibration
