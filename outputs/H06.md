# H06 — AFS Spectroscopy Assessment

## Customer Enquiry

We read the supplied LED NIR kidney study and want to use the same concept to guide tumor boundary decisions during live surgery. Can we quote its findings as proof that the proposed sensor is ready for that use? We have no new in vivo data and no validation on our own setup. What assessment and next feasibility steps are appropriate?

---

## Requirements & Key Facts

**What the client stated:**
- They have read the LED NIR kidney tumour study [S03]
- They want to use the same concept for guiding tumour boundary decisions during live surgery
- They want to quote the study's findings as proof the sensor is ready for surgical use
- They have no new in vivo data
- They have no validation on their own setup

**Key unknowns:**
- Which tumour type / organ system is the actual target (kidney as in S03, or another tissue)?
- What decision the sensor would support (resection margin, tissue classification, other)?
- What regulatory pathway applies (medical device approval, research ethics)?
- What acceptable error rates are for the clinical context (false negative rate in oncology is critical)
- Whether any tissue samples or surgical access for feasibility testing are available

---

## Clarification Questions

1. **Target tissue and tumour type:** Is the intended application kidney tumour (as in S03), or a different organ/tumour type? The S03 study is specific to renal cell carcinoma vs. healthy kidney tissue.

2. **Clinical decision supported:** What specific surgical decision would the sensor inform — resection margin identification, tissue classification, or another function?

3. **Regulatory and ethical framework:** What approval pathway (e.g., CE marking, FDA clearance, research ethics board approval) is required before any in vivo measurements can be performed?

4. **Acceptable error rates:** What false negative and false positive rates would be clinically acceptable? The S03 study explicitly notes high measurement variance in cancer tissue samples [S03].

5. **Sample availability for validation:** Do you have access to excised tissue samples (from pathology) or a pathway to conduct in vivo feasibility measurements under approved protocols?

6. **Sensor hardware:** Would you build a replica of the S03 sensor, use AFS components, or use different hardware entirely? The S03 sensor uses 4 specific NIR LEDs + 1 photodiode — not a full spectrometer.

---

## Recommendation

**Decision Status:** Cannot approve — the S03 study explicitly states it is a proof-of-concept and cannot be cited as validation for clinical/surgical use.

**Reasoning:**

The S03 study's own conclusions state this is a "proof-of-concept" providing only a "first estimate of real-life applicability," with further studies explicitly required [S03]. The study limitations directly contradict readiness for surgical use:

- **Sample size:** 14 in vitro tissue samples from only 7 patients [S03]
- **In vitro only:** All measurements were on excised tissue, not in vivo during surgery [S03]
- **High variance:** The paper explicitly notes high measurement variance in cancer tissue samples [S03]
- **False negatives:** In oncology, false negatives (missing cancer tissue) are clinically unacceptable — the study does not demonstrate acceptable sensitivity for this use case [S03]
- **No clinical validation:** The study is a laboratory demonstration, not a validated clinical tool [S03]

**Quoting S03 as proof of surgical readiness would misrepresent the study's own stated scope and limitations.**

This is not a matter of missing information — it is a fundamental mismatch between what the study demonstrates and what surgical use requires. No amount of clarification changes this fact.

---

## Measurement Concept

**Not applicable at this stage.**

Before any measurement concept can be proposed for surgical guidance, the following sequence must occur:

1. Regulatory/ethics approval for in vivo or ex vivo human tissue measurements
2. A properly powered validation study on the target tissue type
3. Clinical acceptance criteria defined with surgical and oncology stakeholders

**If a research pathway is pursued:**
- The S03 sensor design (4 NIR LEDs at specific wavelengths + photodiode) is a compact concept, but it is NOT a full spectrometer — it samples only 4 discrete wavelengths [S03]
- Fiber link: For NIR wavelengths used in S03, standard Vis-NIR silica fiber (350–2400 nm) would be compatible [S02 p.6-7]
- AFS multi-spectral lab testing could compare the 4-LED approach against full NIR or Raman spectra on excised tissue samples — this would be a research step, not clinical validation

---

## Risks & Uncertainty

- **The S03 study does not validate surgical use** — citing it as such would be scientifically and potentially legally inappropriate [S03]
- **High measurement variance in cancer tissue** is explicitly noted in S03 — this variance may cause unacceptable false negative rates in surgical margin decisions [S03]
- **In vitro ≠ in vivo:** Blood perfusion, tissue hydration, surgical field conditions, and instrument contact differ fundamentally from excised tissue on a bench [general spectroscopy knowledge, not in AFS sources]
- **7 patients / 14 samples** is insufficient for any clinical claim — sample size for medical device validation is determined by regulatory requirements, not convenience [general knowledge]
- **Different tumour types or organs** would require entirely separate validation — kidney tissue results do not transfer to other tissues
- **Regulatory risk:** Using unvalidated devices for surgical decisions may expose patients and practitioners to harm and liability

---

## Feasibility Plan

**The appropriate path is a research validation study, not deployment.**

### If pursuing a research pathway:

1. **Samples:** Obtain excised tissue samples (tumour and healthy margin) from pathology under appropriate ethics approval. The S03 study used 14 samples from 7 patients — a meaningful pilot would require substantially more samples to characterise variance and establish sensitivity/specificity with confidence intervals. The exact number depends on the regulatory pathway and clinical acceptance criteria.

2. **Reference measurements:** Histopathology (gold standard for tumour vs. healthy tissue classification). This is independent of the optical measurement and provides ground truth for supervised model training.

3. **Measurements to compare:**
   - Replicate the S03 4-LED concept on your tissue type
   - Compare against full NIR spectroscopy (e.g., AFS NIR diffuse reflection probe) to determine if additional wavelengths improve discrimination
   - Optionally compare Raman (785 nm excitation) or fluorescence if fluorescent markers are relevant [S01 p.11-12, 15-17]

4. **Sequence:**
   - Single-method models first (4-LED, full NIR, Raman individually)
   - Combined models only if single methods are insufficient and sampling geometry allows [S01 p.14-15]

5. **Go/no-go basis:** Define acceptable sensitivity and specificity thresholds with clinical stakeholders BEFORE the study begins. The acceptance criterion must reflect clinical requirements (e.g., ≤X% false negatives). Do not invent these numbers — they must be agreed with the surgical and oncology team.

6. **Minimum samples:** The required sample count depends on the variance observed (S03 notes high variance in cancer tissue), the number of tissue types, and the regulatory standard. A pilot with too few samples cannot validate a calibration or establish clinical performance.

**What this would demonstrate:**
- Whether the 4-LED concept discriminates your target tissue type with acceptable accuracy
- Whether full spectroscopy outperforms the simplified sensor
- Variance and repeatability on your sample population

**What this would NOT demonstrate:**
- In vivo performance during surgery
- Generalisability to other tumour types or patient populations
- Regulatory approval or clinical readiness
