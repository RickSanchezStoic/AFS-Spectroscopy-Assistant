# H04 — AFS Spectroscopy Assessment

## Customer Enquiry

We want to investigate water content in a petroleum line using MIR ATR. The analyzer would be in another building. We already have a silica fiber cable from a visible or NIR measurement setup and would like to reuse it. The link length, routing and operating conditions have not yet been supplied. Can the assistant approve this concept?

- Proposed method: MIR ATR
- Analyzer location: separate building from measurement point
- Existing asset: silica fiber cable (from visible or NIR setup) — client wishes to reuse

## Recommendation

**Decision Status:** Cannot approve — silica fiber is incompatible with MIR ATR

**Explanation:**

The client's core premise is physically incorrect. Standard silica fiber (whether UV-Vis or Vis-NIR grades) **cannot transmit mid-infrared radiation**. According to [S02 p.6–10]:

- UV-Vis silica fibers transmit approximately 190–1200 nm
- Vis-NIR silica fibers transmit approximately 350–2400 nm (i.e., up to ~2.4 µm)
- MIR ATR operates in the range 4000–400 cm⁻¹, corresponding to **2.5–25 µm**

The water O–H stretching band (~3300–3600 cm⁻¹ ≈ 2.8–3.0 µm) and combination bands (~2300 cm⁻¹ ≈ 4.3 µm) used for water detection in petroleum [S02 p.13] are **entirely outside the silica transmission window**.

**The existing silica fiber cable cannot be reused for this application.**

**Correct fiber requirement:**
For MIR ATR with a remote analyzer, the fiber link must use MIR-compatible fibers [S02 p.6, 9–10]:
- **PIR (polycrystalline AgHal) fibers:** 3–17 µm range; preferred for ATR probes due to high numerical aperture (NA >0.3), giving better coupling with FTIR spectrometers
- **Chalcogenide (CIR) fibers:** 1.1–6.5 µm range; covers the water detection region
- **Hollow glass waveguides (HWG):** 2–18 µm range; but low NA (~0.1) causes poor FTIR coupling — PIR preferred [S02 p.10]

**MIR ATR for water in petroleum is a supported application** [S01 p.6, S02 p.13], but only with appropriate MIR fiber infrastructure.

---

## Measurement Concept

**Probe type:** MIR ATR probe with diamond or ZrO₂ crystal tip, sealed in chemically resistant housing (e.g., PEEK shaft and tubing) [S02 p.13]

**Fiber link:** PIR (silver halide) fiber cable — required for MIR transmission to remote FTIR analyzer [S02 p.9–10]. Fiber length, routing, and environmental protection must be specified based on site survey.

**Measurement location:** In-line or at-line in the petroleum stream; probe in direct contact with product.

**Analyzer location:** FTIR spectrometer in separate building — this is a standard AFS remote configuration [S02 p.13].

*Note: Specific probe model codes, fiber lengths, and component ratings require AFS confirmation after site details are provided.*

---

## Risks & Uncertainty

- **Fiber length and signal attenuation:** PIR fibers have higher attenuation than silica; maximum practical length depends on spectral region and signal-to-noise requirements — this must be assessed once distance is known
- **Temperature limits:** PIR and chalcogenide fibers have specific operating temperature ranges; routing through hot or cold zones may require thermal protection
- **Hazardous area classification:** If the process area is Ex-rated, fiber and probe housings must meet appropriate safety standards — not addressed in current sources
- **Probe fouling:** Petroleum streams may deposit residues on the ATR crystal; cleaning or automatic wipe systems may be needed [S02 p.14 notes fouling as a concern in similar applications]
- **Matrix variation:** Different petroleum grades may shift baseline or interfere with water bands — calibration must cover expected product range
- **Reference method agreement:** Karl Fischer or equivalent must be confirmed as the independent reference

---

## Feasibility Plan

**1. Samples:**
Collect petroleum samples spanning the expected water concentration range (e.g., 0.1% to 2.0% or as specified) and representing typical matrix variation (different batches, grades, or suppliers).

**2. Reference measurements:**
Karl Fischer titration (or equivalent independent method) for each sample — provides water content values independent of MIR spectroscopy.

**3. Measurements to compare:**
- MIR ATR spectra of all samples using AFS Multi-Spectral Fiber System [S05]
- Identify water-related spectral features (O–H stretch ~3300–3600 cm⁻¹, combination band ~2300 cm⁻¹) [S02 p.13]
- Build PLS regression model: MIR spectra vs. Karl Fischer reference values

**4. Sequence:**
- Single-method MIR ATR model first
- If additional analytes or backup method needed, NIR can be tested in parallel (NIR can detect water via O–H overtones, but with lower selectivity in petroleum matrix)

**5. Go/no-go basis:**
Define acceptance criteria with client before testing — e.g., prediction error (RMSEP) relative to process tolerance, correlation coefficient, bias. The specific threshold must be agreed; it is not specified in the sources.

**6. Minimum samples:**
Sample count depends on water range, matrix variability, and desired model robustness. A pilot with too few samples cannot validate calibration transfer to the process. AFS chemometric guidance should be followed [S05].

**What this experiment demonstrates:**
- Whether MIR ATR can quantify water in the specific petroleum matrix
- Which spectral features are diagnostic
- Baseline for in-line probe and fiber system design

**What this experiment does NOT demonstrate:**
- Long-term probe stability or fouling behaviour in continuous process
- Fiber attenuation over the actual installed route (requires site-specific test)
- Compliance with hazardous area requirements (requires separate assessment)
