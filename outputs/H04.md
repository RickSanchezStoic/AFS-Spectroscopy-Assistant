# H04 — Reuse of a Silica Link for Petroleum MIR ATR

**Customer Enquiry:**
> We want to investigate water content in a petroleum line using MIR ATR. The analyzer would be in another building. We already have a silica fiber cable from a visible or NIR measurement setup and would like to reuse it. The link length, routing and operating conditions have not yet been supplied. Can the assistant approve this concept?

---

## Requirements & Key Facts

**Stated by client:**
- Target analyte: water content in a petroleum stream
- Measurement method requested: MIR ATR
- Analyzer location: a separate building (remote installation, long fiber link)
- Existing hardware: a silica fiber cable from a visible or NIR setup
- Link length, routing, and operating conditions: not yet supplied

**Key unknowns:**
- Fiber link length and routing path
- Operating conditions: temperature, vibration, chemical exposure along the cable route
- Process conditions at the measurement point: pressure, temperature, flow rate, chemical composition of the petroleum stream
- Probe access, installation geometry, and hazardous area classification
- Whether any MIR-compatible fiber and ATR probe are available or budgeted separately

1. What is the fiber cable length and routing between the measurement point and the analyzer building?
2. What are the operating conditions along the cable route: temperature range, mechanical stress, and chemical environment?

## Recommendation

**Decision status: Cannot approve the stated concept. A fundamental technical incompatibility must be resolved before any further assessment.**

### The silica fiber is not suitable for MIR ATR

Silica (SiO₂) optical fiber transmits light in the visible and near-infrared range. It does not transmit mid-infrared wavelengths. The MIR ATR method operates in the spectral range of approximately 4000–600 cm⁻¹ (roughly 2.5–17 µm wavelength). Silica fiber absorbs strongly across this range and cannot carry the MIR signal from the process to a remote analyzer [S02 p.6–10].

Reusing the existing silica cable for a MIR ATR application is not technically feasible, regardless of cable length, routing, or operating conditions.

### What fiber is required for MIR ATR remote installations

MIR-compatible fiber types — such as chalcogenide glass fiber or silver halide fiber — are required for transmitting mid-infrared light over a fiber link to a remote analyzer [S02 p.6–10]. These materials have different handling, bending radius, chemical compatibility, and cost characteristics compared to silica fiber. The suitability of a specific MIR fiber type for the cable length, routing, and operating conditions described must be confirmed with AFS.

### Water in petroleum via MIR ATR: the method is supported

Setting aside the fiber incompatibility, MIR ATR is a supported method for water content measurement in petroleum streams [S01 p.6, S02 p.13]. Water has strong, characteristic mid-infrared absorption bands that are well-suited to ATR detection. The application is referenced in the AFS process control materials [S02 p.13].

## Measurement Concept

The concept can be developed once the fiber incompatibility is resolved:

- **ATR probe** at the process measurement point: direct contact with the petroleum stream; probe material and crystal type must be compatible with the petroleum composition, temperature, and pressure [S01 p.6, S02 p.13]
- **MIR-compatible fiber link** (chalcogenide or silver halide fiber) from the probe to the remote analyzer building; length, bend radius, and environmental ratings to be specified with AFS based on the actual routing [S02 p.6–10]
- **MIR analyzer** in the remote building, connected via the MIR fiber link
- The existing silica fiber cable cannot be repurposed for this link; it may remain in service for any separate VIS or NIR measurement but serves no role in the MIR ATR setup

---

## Risks & Uncertainty

- **Fundamental incompatibility confirmed:** the silica fiber cable cannot be reused; this is not a marginal risk but a physical impossibility based on silica's optical transmission properties [S02 p.6–10]
- **MIR fiber handling:** chalcogenide and silver halide fibers are more fragile and have stricter bending and environmental limits than silica; the routing conditions must be assessed before specifying a MIR fiber link
- **Long-distance MIR transmission:** signal attenuation over long fiber links in the MIR is a practical limitation; link length, attenuation per metre, and the analyzer's sensitivity must be confirmed before the remote installation is designed
- **Probe compatibility:** the ATR crystal and probe body must be rated for the petroleum stream's temperature, pressure, and chemical composition; these conditions have not been supplied
- **Hazardous area:** petroleum installations typically require ATEX/IECEx-rated equipment; this has not been addressed
- **No performance figures stated:** water detection limit, accuracy, and update rate depend on the actual configuration and cannot be quoted without experimental data

## Feasibility Plan

**Objective:** Establish whether a remote MIR ATR measurement of water in the petroleum stream is technically feasible with an appropriate MIR fiber link, and define the requirements for equipment selection.

**Step 1 — Supply missing information**
Provide fiber link length, routing conditions (temperature, bending constraints, chemical exposure), process conditions at the probe (pressure, temperature, petroleum composition), and hazardous area classification.

**Step 2 — MIR fiber link assessment**
With AFS, review MIR-compatible fiber options (chalcogenide, silver halide) for the specified link length and routing conditions. Confirm attenuation, bend radius, and environmental ratings.

**Step 3 — Laboratory proof of concept**
Collect petroleum samples with known water content (covering the relevant range). Record MIR ATR spectra in the laboratory on a benchtop ATR platform to confirm that water signals are detectable and quantifiable at the required concentration range.

**Step 4 — Reference method alignment**
Agree on the reference water determination method (e.g. Karl Fischer titration) and establish independent reference values for calibration.

**Step 5 — Remote installation scoping**
Once laboratory feasibility is confirmed and MIR fiber options are defined, scope the remote installation with AFS: probe, fiber link, analyzer housing, hazardous area ratings, and maintenance access.

**Go / no-go basis:** Detectable and quantifiable water signal in laboratory MIR ATR spectra at the required concentration range, and confirmed availability of a MIR fiber link suitable for the installation conditions.
